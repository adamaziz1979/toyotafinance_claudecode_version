Executive Summary
Toyota Finance Australia is the country's largest car financier: a $33.9bn balance sheet that made a record $493.1m net profit in FY26 and now finances four brands, fleets and KINTO subscriptions. Growth has outpaced its governance. Revenue has more than doubled since FY22, headcount is up 23%, and TFA faces two class actions and an ASIC review of car lending that expects lenders to monitor borrower outcomes. The Data Office is hiring a practice leader to establish BI standards, governance and a community of practice, on Power BI and Databricks, with a clear mandate for the appropriate use of AI. The job is to turn scattered reporting into one trusted, governed system that the business can build on without the four-person team becoming the bottleneck.

Stage 1: One-page Executive Briefing

Company in one paragraph

Observed Fact Toyota Finance Australia (TFA) is Toyota's captive finance company in Australia. It is unlisted, sits within Toyota Financial Services and is backed by credit support agreements from Toyota Motor Corporation. It funds itself through global bond, commercial paper and securitisation markets. It earns interest and rental income from retail car loans, dealer floorplan (bailment), fleet leasing and management, and fees from insurance it distributes but does not underwrite. Through its subsidiary AAAF it also runs white-label finance for Mazda and Suzuki, and it operates the KINTO car-share, rental and subscription brands. Customers are consumers, businesses, government fleets and about 740 dealer points across Toyota, Lexus, Mazda, Suzuki and Power Alliance. The 2030 strategy has three pillars: Transform the Ownership Experience, Make usership easy and accessible and Become a future-fit organisation.

Why the Role Exists Now

Strong Inference, medium-high confidence
TFA appears to be formalising a BI practice layer on top of reporting that grew team by team. Four things point that way:
  • Growth outpaced governance. Revenue +127% and FTE +23% since FY22. The JD asks for someone to "establish" standards, governance and an operating framework, which suggests these are not yet mature.
  • Federated delivery. The JD's aim is "empowering teams across the business to deliver reporting... within a governed framework". The Data Office wants to govern that work, not own all of it.
  • The regulator raised the bar. ASIC expects lenders to monitor borrower outcomes, and the class actions test historical data. Trusted reporting is now a risk control.
  • Working Hypothesis Platform and AI timing. The JD names Databricks and "the appropriate use of AI", and TFA's risk factors flag the data-reliability risks of legacy system upgrades.

Stage 3: Top 5 Experiences to Lead With

The interviewers will probably want proof of three things: you can set standards that make numbers trusted, you can enable the business to self-serve without losing control, and you can build a practice and community, not just a team. Each of these five covers a different part of that.

1

Trusted KPIs and BI standards (Cisco, Data Solutions & Insights Lead)

Why it matters here: The JD's core ask is to "establish BI standards, governance frameworks" and "champion reporting quality, consistency". This is the closest match to the real business problem.
STeams saw different numbers for the same KPI (1 to 5% gaps), causing 3 to 5 escalations a month. TGet to zero inconsistency and restore confidence in the numbers. AMoved logic from Power BI into shared SQL single-source views. Added catalogues with plain-English definitions, master dashboards per business area, a Workspace Health dashboard and tripwire alerts for variances over 2%. R27 KPIs from 62% to 100% consistency. Dashboards from 70+ to 46. Escalations from 3 to 5 a month to zero. Workspace health from 60% to 93%.
"The first thing I fix is whether people trust the number. At Cisco the same KPI showed different values depending on which dashboard you opened."

3

Resilient data foundations: lineage, catalogue and release cycles (Cisco, Snowflake and DBT)

Why it matters here: "Reporting lifecycle management", governance and a Databricks-era platform (Working Hypothesis) need exactly these controls. It also answers TFA's own risk factor on data reliability during system upgrades.
SAfter the Snowflake migration, untracked table changes caused rework and hard-to-trace KPI errors; external access risked outages for 200+ stakeholders. TBuild a scalable, controlled architecture based on outcomes, maintainability and cost. AIntroduced a DBT transformation layer with lineage and DBT Docs cataloguing, a GitHub release cycle (development, peer review, tests, production) and secure AWS S3 data sharing. Ran regular data purges. RWarehouse volume down 42% (355 to 205 tables/views, 517.2M stagnant rows removed). Warehouse disruptions from 1 to 2 a quarter to zero.
